Fact vs Fiction

Mental Health: Get the Facts

Myth: Mental health problems only affect adults.
Fiction: Mental health challenges can affect people of all ages, including kids and teens. Early support can make a big difference over time.
Myth: If you feel sad or anxious, something is wrong with you.
Fiction: Feeling sad or anxious sometimes is part of being human. It doesn't mean you're broken— it may mean you're stressed, overwhelmed, or need support.
Myth: Mental health isn't as important as physical health.
Fact: Mental health affects how you think, feel, and act—just like physical health affects your body. Both matter, and both deserve care.
Myth: You can usually tell when someone is struggling.
Fiction: Many people hide what they're going through—even from close friends and family. A kind check-in can mean a lot.
Myth: Talking about mental health doesn't help.
Fact: Opening up to someone you trust can reduce loneliness, ease pressure, and help you feel supported. You don't have to carry everything alone.
Myth: Strong people don't get affected by mental health.
Fiction: Mental health challenges can affect anyone—no matter how strong, successful, or "put together" they seem. Getting help is a smart move, not a weakness.
Myth: A mental health challenge is a sign of weakness.
Fiction: Many people come out of tough seasons with stronger coping skills, more self-awareness, and better support systems than before.
